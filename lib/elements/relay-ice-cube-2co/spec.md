# relay-ice-cube-2co · v1.8 · plug-in control relay DPDT 15 A, 24 V DC, on its DIN socket

**What** An 8-pin plug-in "ice cube" relay with two changeover poles, a 24 V DC coil, a push-to-test button, a lock-down slider, a mechanical flag and an LED, in a clear case, on its 35 mm DIN socket with eight screw terminals. It is the **first element built mechanism-first**: magnet system, return spring, actuator card and contacts are real geometry and nodes, and the case wraps them.
**Class** device · **Baseline** the 8-pin DPDT plug-in relay and its 8-pin DIN socket, maker's datasheet (references/interior.md: relay specification p. 1, 3, 5, 7; socket drawing p. 1; STEP read-back of the pin field and envelope). The maker's name and part numbers are only in references/ (OPERATING.md 6). Nothing of a vendor file ships.
**Units / frame** mm, Z up. Origin: rail device: the socket's back face on the rail crown y = 0, rail centreline z = 0, socket left face x = 0, body toward −y. The relay's base plane is the socket's front face (y = −29.7). Below, "relay-centred" x and z are the relay's own frame (x 0 at the case centre, which sits at socket x +14.6).
**Built with** toolkit 1.3 · AutoLISP command `RELAY` · parameters `view` (product | cutaway), `socket` (true), `operated` (false: the pose the glb is written in; the twin moves the nodes).
**Data with it** `parts.csv` (the sub-element tree, models and parameters), `behaviour.md` (states, transitions, failure modes, grading hooks), `references/interior.md` (the mechanism brief with every figure's page).

## Named nodes (the twin's view of the mechanism)
`coil` (emissive in x-ray while energised) · `armature` (rotate about x at the hinge, 0–4.06°) · `actuator` (translate +y, 0–1.2) · `spring-return` (**compress** along y with the actuator, 0–1.2: the binder scales the mesh along y) · `pole-1`, `pole-2` (rotate about x at the finger root, 0–7.4°: released on N.C., operated on N.O.) · `btn-test` (translate +y, 0–1.5) · `door-lock` (the lock-down slider: translate +x, 0–4.0) · `flag` (translate +x, 0–3.6, with the actuator) · `led-coil` (emissive) · `case` (fixed; hidden in the cutaway view) · `t-1 … t-14` (eight socket terminals, kind port, unit screw, gender none (screw clamps, PIN-002): the ids connections.csv uses, equal to the relay pin numbers).
Deviation from parts.csv: `actuator` was `fixed` in the brief; it moves, so it is a node here. `spring-return` carries the spring model's state.

## Mechanism as built against behaviour.md's states (review relay-ice-cube-1.1 finding 2)
- Operated (1.3): the armature turns 4.06° and its tip carries the card 1.2; over the core (z +4.5 … +8.5) the plate moves 0.52 to 0.24, so the core's pole face now stands at y −36.08 (1.08 proud of the bobbin), where the turned plate meets it: released gap 0.52 over the core, operated 0 at the core's lower edge, opening to 0.28 at its upper edge (the plate's 4.06° across the Ø4 face). The pose check reads 0.025 (its 21-point sample of the plate). 1.2 stopped 0.68 to 0.96 short; core watch's elec-4 answer item 3 made the check measure the face over the core, and the states table says "at the core".
- Manual test (1.3): a card tab (row 12) sits 0.26 behind the button's inner face. The button's 1.5 takes up the 0.26 and pushes the card its 1.2 (lib/motion.csv btn-test → actuator, the core's row from request elec-4; MOT-020). The armature does not follow the button (elec-4 answer item 1): in the manual test the card and the contacts move and the armature stays where the coil leaves it.
- Locked test (1.5): a nib on the slider's +x end (row 21) reaches back through a slot in the face to the pressed button's front (y −37.36). With the button pressed its 1.5 and the slider at its range end (4.0), the nib stands over the button (x +4.84 … +6.34 of +4.14 … +8.05) with its back on the button's front, so the button and the card it drives are held in (relay specification p. 1; behaviour.md "locked test"). With the button at rest the nib meets it at 45 % of the slide: the real part's interlock, the slider closes only over a pressed button. lib/motion.csv (the core's, request elec-6) declares it, `btn-test → door-lock requires 1.5`: MOT-010 sweeps the slider with the button held pressed. 1.4's slider lay on the face, about 1.8 in front of the pressed button, and could not hold it. A difference on file stays: the STEP shows the slider recessed 0.13 / 0.18 in the face (interior.md:73), where the model lays it 0.5 in front of it.
- The pose check is `python lib/build.py poses relay-ice-cube-2co` (toolkit/poses.py, the core's, elec-4 answer item 2): every node posed by its own pivot's range end. 1.3: 23 of 23 agree. Since core watch's escape group 24 (2026-11-07) the locked row is bounded on both sides (toolkit/poses.holds), so 1.3's and 1.4's file read 22 of 23 (the locked row); 1.5's result is gate/relay-ice-cube-2co-poses.json (round 18 report).

## Notes
Interior geometry is sized from principle inside the cited envelope; the twin's behaviour comes from `parts.csv` (coil R 640 Ω, pull-in 19.2 V, drop-out 2.4 V, contact ratings, life) through `lib/models.md`, not from the geometry. The geometry's job is to make the cause visible: which part moved, which contact is touching, which spring is weak. The ratings are not yet in a ratings.csv (review finding 4, class A, EM's): lib/needs-em/elec.md r13.
